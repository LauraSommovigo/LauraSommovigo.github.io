Research

Dust across scales and wavelengths

JWST sees UV-bright, apparently dust-free galaxies at z > 10, while ALMA finds dust-rich systems only a few hundred million years later. Does this need new galaxy-formation physics, or does it follow once dust is modelled consistently, from single grains to whole galaxy populations?

One picture, from grains to galaxy populations

Dust absorbs, scatters and re-emits starlight, so it shapes nearly everything we measure about a galaxy. The physics is set at very different scales. Grain size and composition fix the extinction curve. Cloud structure turns it into an attenuation curve and sets the dust temperature. Galaxy geometry makes attenuation depend on viewing angle. Galaxy populations decide how dust builds up and survives across cosmic time. I model each link and connect them, so JWST's UV view and ALMA's infrared view of the same galaxies can be read with one consistent physical picture.

UV–optical · what JWST sees

  • Dust dims and reddens starlight. Attenuation curves, UV slopes and UV luminosity functions all carry its imprint, mixed with geometry and viewing angle.

Far-infrared · what ALMA sees

  • The absorbed energy comes out as thermal dust emission. Dust masses, temperatures and infrared luminosities measure what the UV misses.

Methods: analytical models, radiative transfer, cosmological simulations, synthetic observations and interpretable machine learning, tested against the ALMA and JWST programmes I help lead.

microns · dust grains

Who makes the first dust, and what is it made of?

Grain size and composition set the intrinsic extinction curve, the starting point for everything downstream. How grains are produced and reprocessed changes over cosmic time.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • Evolving grain sizes. A live dust model that evolves dust mass and grain-size distribution shows when small grains, scattering and viewing angle control the attenuation curve. Matsumoto, Sommovigo+26
  • Dust mixtures. Milky Way, SMC and large-grain dust compared across thousands of simulated galaxies. Sommovigo+25

Far-infrared · emission

  • Supernova dust. Dust-to-stellar mass ratios of about 10−3 in REBELS and ALPINE galaxies point to supernova dust production, possibly helped by grain growth. Sommovigo+22a 22b
parsecs · star-forming clouds

How much starlight stays hidden in its birth cloud?

In star-forming regions, turbulence, gas density and the geometry of dust and stars turn the extinction curve into an effective attenuation curve and set the dust temperature.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • Hidden starlight. Compact clouds at high redshift disperse slowly, so about 40% of young stars' UV light stays obscured. UV and infrared emission need not come from the same place. Sommovigo+20
  • Clouds over their lifetime. UV and infrared radiation followed through the turbulent life of simulated molecular clouds. Di Mascia, Pallottini, Sommovigo+25

Far-infrared · emission

  • Warm dust. An analytical cloud model predicts dust warmer than 60 K in z ∼ 8 galaxies, since confirmed by ALMA. Sommovigo+20
kiloparsecs · galaxies

What do attenuation and dust emission tell us about a galaxy?

Across a galaxy, varying column density, multiphase gas and the star–dust geometry make attenuation depend strongly on viewing angle, while dust emission stays nearly isotropic.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • Priors for JWST attenuation curves. Radiative transfer on IllustrisTNG50 and TNG100 galaxies gives physically motivated attenuation curves for SED fitting. Sommovigo+25
  • An interpretable attenuation law. Machine-learning compression and symbolic regression give a four-parameter form tied to galaxy properties. Sommovigo+26b

Far-infrared · emission

  • Dust from a single ALMA band. Dust temperature and mass from one continuum point plus [Cii], applied to the REBELS and ALPINE surveys. Sommovigo+21
  • Multi-temperature dust. Single-temperature fits can miss dust mass and infrared luminosity by up to ∼1 dex. Sommovigo & Algera 25
megaparsecs · galaxy populations

Do early galaxies need new physics, or better dust?

Across cosmic time, galaxy populations decide how dust builds up, gets ejected and survives. This is where models meet the statistics of JWST and ALMA surveys.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • Blue Monsters and dusty descendants. A forward model linking dust physics to UV and infrared luminosity functions from z ≈ 7 to 14. A porous, turbulent ISM eases the UV–IR tension. Sommovigo+26a
  • Where has the dust gone? Dust ejection can explain blue, UV-bright galaxies at z > 10. Ferrara, Pallottini & Sommovigo 25

Far-infrared · emission

  • Cosmic dust temperature. The first physical model for how dust temperature evolves: dust runs warmer at early times as gas depletion times shorten. Sommovigo+22a

Learning the Universe

Co-lead, Synthetic Observations Working Group

A Simons Foundation collaboration that uses simulations and machine learning to infer galaxy-formation physics from surveys. My working group turns simulations into mock observations that can be compared directly with data.
