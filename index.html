Laura Sommovigo

Learning the Universe Research Fellow, Department of Astronomy, Columbia University, New York

I am an astrophysicist studying how galaxies formed and evolved in the first billion years of cosmic history, with a focus on the physics of dust. I combine ALMA and JWST observations with physically motivated models and numerical simulations to understand how dust forms, evolves and reshapes what we observe of early galaxies. I develop multi-scale frameworks that connect ISM physics to galaxy-scale observables, using radiative transfer, synthetic observations and forward modelling. I am co-PI and theory lead of the ALMA Large Program PHOENIX, co-lead the Synthetic Observations Working Group of the Learning the Universe theory collaboration, and am a co-investigator on more than 30 successful ALMA, JWST and Roman proposals.

One picture, from grains to galaxy populations

Dust absorbs, scatters and re-emits starlight, so it shapes nearly every observable property of a galaxy. The relevant physics spans many scales. Grain composition and size distribution set the extinction curve. In star-forming clouds, turbulence and the dust–star geometry turn extinction into attenuation and set the dust temperature. Across a galaxy, attenuation depends on viewing angle. Over cosmic time, dust production, growth, destruction and ejection set the dust content of galaxy populations. I model each scale and connect them, so that UV and infrared observations of the same galaxies can be interpreted consistently.

Rest-frame UV–optical

  • Dust dims and reddens starlight. Attenuation curves, UV slopes and UV luminosity functions carry its imprint, but also depend on geometry and viewing angle.

Rest-frame far-infrared

  • The absorbed energy is re-emitted as thermal dust emission. The far-infrared continuum constrains dust temperature, dust mass and dust-obscured star formation.

Putting it together

In Sommovigo+26a, grain properties and ISM structure set, through radiative transfer, both the UV attenuation and the infrared emission of each galaxy. Galaxy populations then test the combination against the observed UV and infrared luminosity functions.

microns · dust grains

Who makes the first dust, and what is it made of?

Grain composition and size distribution set the intrinsic extinction curve. Both depend on how dust is produced and reprocessed in the interstellar medium, which can change over cosmic time.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • How do evolving grain sizes shape the attenuation curve? Simulations that evolve dust mass and grain-size distribution show when small grains, scattering, viewing angle or unobscured young stars set the shape of the attenuation curve. Matsumoto, Sommovigo+26
  • Can one attenuation law describe different grain mixtures? A new functional form for the attenuation curve fits Milky Way, SMC and large-grain dust alike. Comparing its parameters separates the effects of galaxy properties and viewing angle from those of grain properties, and identifies the most informative parameter combinations. Sommovigo & Bartlett+26b

Far-infrared · emission

  • What produced the dust in galaxies at z ≈ 5–7? Dust-to-stellar mass ratios of ∼3×10−3 in REBELS and ALPINE galaxies require grain growth in the ISM on top of supernova dust; growth may dominate, depending on how much supernova dust survives the reverse shock. Sommovigo+22a 22b
parsecs · star-forming clouds

How much starlight stays hidden in its birth cloud?

In star-forming clouds, the turbulent gas structure and the relative geometry of dust and stars turn the extinction curve into an attenuation curve and set the dust temperature.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • How long do newborn stars stay hidden? Compact high-redshift clouds are dispersed more slowly by stellar feedback, so ∼40% of the UV light from newborn stars remains obscured. UV and infrared emission need not be co-spatial. Sommovigo+20
  • Can a 1D analytical model capture the essentials of 3D radiative transfer? We compare SKIRT radiative transfer on simulated turbulent clouds with a 1D analytical model. The two broadly agree on infrared luminosity, SED shape, dust temperatures (means within ∼10–20 K) and the radial UV optical depth, while the 3D simulations also reveal optically thin channels through which 0.1–10% of UV photons escape. Di Mascia, Pallottini, Sommovigo+25

Far-infrared · emission

  • Why is dust warmer in early galaxies? The analytical model predicts dust temperatures above 60 K in compact clouds at z ∼ 8, consistent with temperatures since measured in z ∼ 8 galaxies. Sommovigo+20
kiloparsecs · galaxies

What do attenuation and dust emission tell us about a galaxy?

Across a galaxy, variations in dust column density, multiphase gas and star–dust geometry make attenuation strongly dependent on the line of sight, while dust emission is close to isotropic.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • Which attenuation curves should we assume in SED fitting? Radiative transfer on IllustrisTNG50 and TNG100 galaxies gives physically motivated attenuation-curve priors for SED fitting. Sommovigo+25
  • Which galaxy properties set the attenuation-curve shape? Information-ordered compression and symbolic regression give a new four-parameter attenuation curve, with scaling relations linking its parameters to galaxy properties. Sommovigo & Bartlett+26b Implemented as the SommovigoBartlett2026 class in the Synthesizer dust attenuation module.

Far-infrared · emission

  • What can large samples with single-band dust continuum tell us? Dust temperature and mass from one continuum measurement combined with [Cii] 158 µm, applied to the ALPINE (z ≈ 4–6) and REBELS (z ≈ 6–9) surveys. Sommovigo+21
  • How biased are single-temperature dust fits? With the few continuum bands typically available at high redshift, single-temperature fits can be off by up to ∼1 dex in dust mass, infrared luminosity and obscured star formation. Sommovigo & Algera 25
megaparsecs · galaxy populations

Do early galaxies need new physics, or better dust?

Population statistics, such as UV and infrared luminosity functions, test dust models against the whole galaxy population rather than individual objects.

UV–optical · attenuation

  • Can current dust and galaxy-formation models reproduce both the UV and infrared luminosity functions out to z > 10? A forward model combining the halo mass function, a simple galaxy-formation model, stellar population synthesis and dust prescriptions to predict UV and infrared luminosity functions at z = 7–14. Sommovigo+26a
  • Where has the dust gone in UV-bright galaxies at z > 10? Radiation-driven outflows might clear dust from UV-bright galaxies at z > 10, making them appear blue. In the broader picture, though, outflows are only one of several effects that can produce this, so the interpretation is not straightforward. Ziparo, Ferrara, Sommovigo+23 Ferrara, Pallottini & Sommovigo 25

Far-infrared · emission

  • How does dust temperature evolve across cosmic time? A physical model for how dust temperature evolves with redshift: Td rises mildly with redshift because gas depletion times are shorter at early times. Sommovigo+22a

Collaborations

Theory and observations

My work sits between simulation groups and observing teams, and much of it happens in collaborations.

Observations

PHOENIX

Co-PI and theory lead

ALMA Large Program, with joint JWST time, observing 15 UV-bright galaxies at z > 8 in [O iii] 88 µm and dust continuum. It measures dust-obscured star formation, tests whether supernovae or grain growth build the first dust, and characterizes the ISM. PI: S. Schouws.

Theory & simulation

Learning the Universe

Co-lead, Synthetic Observations Working Group

A Simons Foundation collaboration that combines simulations and machine learning to infer galaxy-formation physics from survey data. The working group I co-lead turns simulations into mock observations that can be compared directly with data.
